## Mental Model First: Tricking the Brain

- **VR is an Oxymoron**: It's a "virtual" (in essence, not in fact) "reality" (actually existing). To make it work, we don't need to match the real world perfectly; we just need to provide enough artificial stimulation to trigger a sense of presence.
- **The Core Goal**: Inducing targeted behavior in an organism. We want to "fool" the brain into feeling present in a world that isn't there.
- **The Brain is Adaptable**: It doesn't need 8K graphics to feel "there." It needs low latency, consistent tracking, and correct proprioception.

---

## 1. Defining VR & AR

### Virtual Reality (VR)
"Inducing targeted behavior in an organism by using artificial sensory stimulation, while the organism has little or no awareness of the interference." (LaValle)

- **Organism**: Not just humans! VR has been used for fruit flies, rodents, and even monkeys.
- **Awareness**: The moment you notice the headset's weight or a lag in the visual, the "illusion" breaks.

### Augmented Reality (AR)
Unlike VR, which replaces your vision, AR overlays virtual information onto the physical environment.
- **Azuma's Criteria**: To be true AR, it must:
  1. Combine real and virtual content.
  2. Be interactive in real-time.
  3. Be registered in 3D (the virtual object stays locked in 3D space relative to the real world).

## 2. Immersion vs. Presence (Slater and Wilbur)

This is the most critical distinction in the field:

- **Immersion (Objective)**: The technological degree to which a VR system projects stimuli onto the sensory receptors (FOV, Resolution, Latency, Audio).
- **Presence (Subjective)**: The psychological "sense of being there." Presence is the *outcome* of immersion.

### The 4 Illusions of Presence
To achieve a high degree of presence, a system must sustain:
1. **The Illusion of Being in a Stable Place**: The world doesn't move when your head moves.
2. **The Illusion of Self-Embodiment**: You see a virtual body (avatar) where yours should be.
3. **The Illusion of Physical Interaction**: You can touch, move, and collide with virtual objects.
4. **The Illusion of Social Communication**: Other agents in the world respond to you as if you are there.

---

## 3. Mixed Reality Continuum (Milgram)

Reality is not binary. It's a spectrum:
`Reality` $\to$ `Augmented Reality` $\to$ `Augmented Virtuality` $\to$ `Virtual Reality`.

- **Augmented Virtuality (AV)**: Bringing real-world elements into a virtual world (e.g., a real keyboard shown in a VR office).
- **Mediated Reality**: Any technology that adds, removes, or alters our perception of the real world.
- **Diminished Reality**: A subset of AR that *removes* real-world elements from your view (e.g., digitally "erasing" a cluttered desk).

---

## 4. History: Milestones of Perception

- **1832: Wheatstone's Stereoscope**: Proved that binocular disparity (two slightly different images for each eye) is how humans perceive 3D depth.
- **1960: Heilig's Stereoscopic Television**: The first actual HMD patent.
- **1962: Sensorama**: A full-body simulator booth. It had 3D video, sound, wind, vibration, and smells (e.g., "baking bread" or "car exhaust").
- **1968: The Sword of Damocles (Ivan Sutherland)**: The first head-mounted display built in a lab. It was so heavy it had to be suspended from the ceiling to avoid breaking the user's neck.

---

## 5. Fidelity & The Uncanny Valley

- **Representational Fidelity**: How photorealistic the world looks.
- **Interaction Fidelity**: How realistically virtual objects respond to your physical actions.
- **Experiential Fidelity**: How well the user's experience matches the creator's intention.

### The Uncanny Valley
As a virtual character becomes more human-like, our empathy increases—until they are "almost" human, at which point our reaction turns to revulsion.
