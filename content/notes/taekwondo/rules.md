Olympic-style sparring (Kyorugi) runs on electronic scoring and a rulebook that changes almost every season. This is the current state of the rules as of 2026, including the batch that landed this year. If you have only ever done point-stop or ITF-style sparring, the WT ruleset is a different game.

---

## (y-) The Format

- **Best of three rounds.** Each round is two minutes, with a one-minute break between them. First to win two rounds wins the match.
- **Golden point.** A tied round goes to a sudden-death fourth round: first point, or first two-point advantage on penalties, wins.
- **The mat.** An 8m octagon. Step off and you get penalized (more on that below).

---

## (y-) Electronic Scoring (PSS)

Scoring runs on the **Protector and Scoring System (PSS)**: an electronic trunk protector (hogu), electronic head guard, and sensor socks. A valid kick or punch has to land with enough force on a valid target for the system to register it. This is why modern sparring looks the way it does, light, flicking, foot-fencing kicks that just trip the sensor rather than heavy knockout blows.

### Point Values

| Technique                     | Points |
| ----------------------------- | ------ |
| Punch to the body             | 1      |
| Kick to the body              | 2      |
| Turning (spinning) kick to body | 4    |
| Kick to the head              | 3      |
| Turning (spinning) kick to head | 5    |

The premium on turning kicks is why the spin-heavy styles I like exist. A single spinning head kick is worth five, more than two clean body kicks. High risk, highest reward.

## (y-) 2026 Rule Changes

Two waves landed this year. The first took effect 1 January 2026, the second was approved at the General Assembly in Tashkent on 11 April 2026 and took effect 1 June 2026 at the Rome Grand Prix.

### Effective 1 January 2026

- **Punch scoring gets sensors.** A semi-automatic scoring method for punches is rolling in, with punch sensors added to the PSS. Punches were historically judged by human referees, which made them unreliable and underused. Instrumenting them is meant to bring the punch back as a real weapon.
- **No coach replay on head kicks.** Coaches can no longer request video replay for head kicks. The center referee may still call for review after counting a strong head kick that failed to score.

### Effective 1 June 2026

- **Wider point gap: 12 to 15.** A round ends early on a scoring gap of 15 points instead of 12. Fewer blowout stoppages, more athletes staying live deeper into a round.
- **Double-value gam-jeom in the last 10 seconds.** A gam-jeom (penalty) for passive behavior in the final ten seconds of a round now gives the opponent **two** points instead of one. Directly targets clock-killing and stalling.
- **Stricter boundary.** A gam-jeom is called if any part of the foot crosses the boundary line, unless it was forced by the opponent's prohibited conduct. Tighter than the old "fully out" standard.
- **Video replay via technical card.** At G-6 events and above, a coach may spend a technical card to challenge a technical error. A failed challenge costs a penalty. Complex final-ten-second actions can go to specific technical review.
- **Ceremony restored.** The commands *charyeot* (attention) and *kyeongnye* (bow) are back before the winner is announced, restoring the traditional bookend to the match.

---

## (y-) Penalties (Gam-jeom)

A gam-jeom awards a point to your opponent (two, now, in the last ten seconds). Common causes:

- Crossing the boundary line
- Falling down
- Avoiding or delaying the match / passive behavior
- Grabbing, pushing, or holding the opponent
- Attacking below the waist, or after "Kal-yeo" (break)
- Turning your back and dropping your guard to avoid a kick

Ten gam-jeom in a single match, and you lose it outright.

---

## (y-) What It Means for Style

- **Pressure pays now.** The double-value stalling penalty and the wider gap both punish the runner and reward the fighter who keeps coming forward. That is the [[notes/taekwondo/sparring|(y-) CJ Nickolas pressure style]] in a nutshell.
- **The punch is coming back.** Once the sensors make it reliable, the straight body punch stops being a novelty and becomes a legit tempo tool to stop forward momentum.
- **Boundary control is a weapon.** With the stricter line, pushing an opponent toward the edge and forcing a foot over is a clean way to bank points without landing a strike.
