Every inch of split range you will ever gain is decided before you sit down into the stretch. A cold hip will not open, no matter how long you sit in it or how much you want it to. This page is the fifteen minutes that come first, structured so the deep work in [[notes/taekwondo/splits-progression|(y-) the splits progression]] actually has something to work with.

The rule is simple: **warm-up is dynamic, cool-down is static**. If you are holding a position and breathing, you are in the wrong half of the session.

---

## (y-) Why Warming Up Buys You Range

Warming up is not a superstition about pulled muscles. Three separate things change, and all three give you range:

- **Muscle temperature.** Warm tissue is more compliant. A muscle at working temperature deforms further under the same load than a cold one, and it does it without the microtrauma.
- **Synovial fluid.** Hip and knee joints only lubricate under movement. Circling and swinging the joint spreads fluid across the surfaces so the head of the femur actually glides in the socket instead of grinding.
- **Nervous system permission.** This is the big one. Your range is capped by a protective reflex, not by tissue length. Movement at progressively larger amplitudes tells the nervous system the range is safe and it releases the brake.

That third point is why a jog alone is a bad warm-up for splits. Raising your heart rate does nothing for hip permission. You have to visit the range you want to use.

---

## (y-) The RAMP Structure

RAMP is the standard sports-science warm-up model: **R**aise, **A**ctivate, **M**obilise, **P**otentiate. It is about 15 minutes and it maps cleanly onto a Taekwondo session.

| Phase      | Time    | Goal                                       |
| ---------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Raise      | 3-4 min | Core temperature and heart rate up         |
| Activate   | 3-4 min | Wake the glutes, core, and hip stabilisers |
| Mobilise   | 5-6 min | Move the hips through full dynamic range   |
| Potentiate | 2-3 min | Sharp, near-maximal kicks to prime the CNS |

---

## (y-) 1. Raise (3 to 4 minutes)

Nothing clever here. You want to break a light sweat and nothing more.

- **Skipping rope or jogging.** 2 minutes, easy pace.
- **High knees and butt kicks.** 30 seconds each. These also start the hip flexor and quad range.
- **Side shuffles.** 30 seconds each direction. First adductor input of the session, gentle.

Stop when you feel warm, not when you feel tired. Fatiguing yourself in the warm-up costs you kick height later.

---

## (y-) 2. Activate (3 to 4 minutes)

This is the step almost everyone skips, and it is the reason side splits stall. Your brain will not permit deep hip range if the pelvis and trunk feel unstable there. Stability first, then range.

- **Glute bridges.** 15 reps, squeeze hard at the top for a second. The glutes are the anchor for every high kick.
- **Deadbugs.** 10 per side, slow. Teaches the trunk to stay braced while the limbs move independently, which is exactly what a head kick is.
- **Clamshells or banded lateral walks.** 15 per side. Wakes the glute medius, the muscle that holds your standing hip level when the other leg goes up.
- **Bird dogs.** 8 per side. Ties the trunk to the hip.

If you only have time for two, take the glute bridges and the deadbugs.

---

## (y-) 3. Mobilise (5 to 6 minutes)

Now visit the range. Every movement is controlled and progressive: start at half amplitude and grow it over the reps. Never bounce, never force, never hold.

| Drill                    | Reps        | What It Opens                         |
| ------------------------ | ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Front-to-back leg swings | 15 per leg  | Hamstrings and hip flexors            |
| Side-to-side leg swings  | 15 per leg  | Adductors and abductors               |
| Standing hip circles     | 10 each way | Whole joint capsule                   |
| Deep lateral lunges      | 10 per side | Adductors, under load                 |
| World's greatest stretch | 6 per side  | Hip flexors, thoracic spine, groin    |
| 90/90 hip switches       | 10 total    | Internal and external hip rotation    |
| Cossack squats           | 8 per side  | Adductors at end range, with strength |

The **90/90 switches** deserve a special mention. Sit with both knees bent at 90 degrees, one leg in front and one to the side, and rotate the whole system to the other side without using your hands. Side splits are gated by hip rotation as much as by adductor length, and this is the cheapest way to train it.

---

## (y-) 4. Potentiate (2 to 3 minutes)

Finish by taking the range you just opened and using it fast, so the nervous system files it under "usable" rather than "stretching".

- **Slow-to-fast kicks.** For each kick (front, roundhouse, side), throw 5 slow and controlled, then 5 sharp. Air only, no bag.
- **Rising height sets.** Three kicks at belt height, three at chest, three as high as you have. Never start at maximum.
- **A single held kick.** Extend one kick at your top height and hold it 3 seconds. If it wobbles badly, your activation phase was too short.

---

## (y-) The 5-Minute Emergency Version

When class starts in five minutes:

1. 60 seconds of skipping or high knees.
2. 15 glute bridges.
3. 15 leg swings per leg, front-back then side-side.
4. 8 deep lateral lunges.
5. 5 rising-height kicks per leg.

It is not the full protocol, but it is the difference between a warm hip and a cold one.

---

## (y-) What Not To Do Before Training

- **No long static holds.** Holding a stretch beyond 60 seconds before explosive work measurably drops power and jump height for up to an hour. Save every static hold for the cool-down.
- **No forcing a split cold.** The split at the start of class is a test, not a stretch, and a cold test is how hamstrings tear at the attachment.
- **No ballistic bouncing.** Bouncing at end range triggers the stretch reflex, which tightens the exact muscle you are trying to open.
- **No maximal effort on the first kick.** The first head kick of the session should never be your hardest.
