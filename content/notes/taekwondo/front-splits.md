The front split is the achievable one. Most people are far closer to it than they think, and it is the split that most directly buys kick height: high front kick, axe kick, and the chamber depth for a back kick all live here. This page is the detail work under [[notes/taekwondo/splits-progression|(y-) the splits progression]], warmed up with [[notes/taekwondo/warmup-routine|(y-) the RAMP protocol]] first.

---

## (y-) What Is Actually Limiting You

A front split is one leg in hip flexion and the other in hip extension. Two different tissues are fighting you, on two different legs, and they need different work.

| Leg       | Position  | Limiter                                       |
| --------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Front leg | Flexion   | Hamstrings, and the sciatic nerve's tolerance |
| Back leg  | Extension | Hip flexors (psoas, rectus femoris), quad     |

Almost everyone attacks only the front leg. If your split is stuck with the front leg flat and the back knee refusing to straighten, your problem is the **back leg**, and no amount of hamstring reaching will fix it.

**The square test.** Set up in your split and look down at your hip bones. If the back hip is rotated open and pointing outward, you are cheating range by turning the split sideways. Square the hips to the front and you will drop several inches higher. That honest, uglier version is the one worth training.

---

## (y-) 1. Front Leg: The Hamstrings

- **Seated forward fold.** One leg extended, one bent. Hinge from the hip, not the spine, and keep the back flat. A rounded spine feels deeper but stretches your lower back instead of the hamstring.
- **Supine leg raise with a strap or belt.** Lie on your back, loop a belt around the arch, and pull the straight leg toward you. The floor holds your pelvis in place, which makes this the cleanest hamstring position there is and the safest one for PNF.
- **Standing hamstring on a chair.** Heel on a chair, leg straight, hinge forward. Toes pointing up hits the middle hamstring, toes turned slightly in hits the outer.
- **Half split (runner's stretch).** From a low lunge, sit the hips back over the rear foot and straighten the front leg. This is the closest position to the real front split and the one to spend the most time in.

### PNF on the hamstring

The supine strap position is the best place to run [[notes/taekwondo/stretching|(y-) contract-relax]]:

1. Pull the leg to a real but comfortable limit.
2. Push the leg back down into the strap at 50 to 70% effort for 8 seconds.
3. Exhale and pull the leg deeper. Hold 20 to 30 seconds.
4. Three to four rounds, each one a little further.

## (y-) 2. Back Leg: The Hip Flexors

This is the neglected half and usually where the real gain is hiding.

- **Couch stretch.** Rear foot elevated against a wall, shin vertical, front foot planted. Now **tuck the pelvis under**, tailbone down. Without that tuck you are just arching your lower back and feeling nothing. With it, this is the most effective hip flexor stretch that exists.
- **Kneeling low lunge with posterior tilt.** Same principle on the floor. Squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg hard. Glute contraction reciprocally inhibits the hip flexor and it lets go.
- **Half-kneeling hip flexor with an overhead reach.** Add a reach and slight side-bend away from the kneeling leg to catch the psoas higher up.

The pelvic tuck is non-negotiable in all three. If your lower back is arching, the stretch has escaped into your spine.

---

## (y-) 3. Nerve Tension vs Muscle Tension

If your "hamstring stretch" feels sharp, electric, or tingly behind the knee and down the calf, that is not muscle. That is sciatic nerve tension, and pulling harder on it makes it angrier, not longer.

**Nerve flossing** is the fix. Lie on your back with the leg raised and knee slightly bent. Now alternate: point the toe and extend the knee, then flex the foot and bend the knee. Ten slow cycles. You are gliding the nerve rather than stretching it. Do this on days the hamstring feels electric rather than tight, and the sharp sensation clears within a couple of weeks.

Muscle tension is a dull, broad pull that eases as you breathe. Nerve tension is sharp, narrow, and does not ease. Learn the difference before you injure something.

## (y-) 4. The Progression Ladder

Work down this list. Only move on when the current step is comfortable for 60 seconds with square hips.

| Stage | Position                                   | Target                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| 1     | Half split, hands on floor                 | Flat back, chest toward shin     |
| 2     | Supported split, hands on blocks or books  | Sink and hold 60s, hips square   |
| 3     | Supported split, forearms down             | Hold 60s without shaking         |
| 4     | Flat split, hips square, torso upright     | The actual front split           |
| 5     | Elevated split, front heel on a low block  | Range past the floor             |
| 6     | Active split entry, lowering under control | Strength through the whole range |

Stage 5 and 6 are where the split turns into kick height. A passive floor split does not automatically raise your kicks; an oversplit and a controlled entry do.

---

## (y-) 5. Turning It Into Kick Height

- **Slow controlled front raises.** Standing, raise a straight leg as high as it will go with zero swing, hold 5 seconds at the top, lower slowly. 8 per leg.
- **Wall-assisted holds.** Hold the leg at maximum height with your hand against a wall, then let go and hold it there for 3 to 5 seconds unaided. This isometric gap between passive and active range is what a floppy high kick is missing.
- **Slow axe kicks.** Raise to full height and lower under control, no drop. The axe kick is the front split standing up.

---

## (y-) 6. A 15-Minute Session

Run this after training, when the tissue is already warm.

1. Half split, 60s per side, easy.
2. Supine strap PNF, 3 rounds per leg.
3. Couch stretch, 90s per side, pelvis tucked.
4. Supported split on blocks, 60s per side, breathing out to sink.
5. Slow leg raises, 8 per leg, to lock the new range in.

Daily is fine at this volume. Alternate the PNF-heavy days with lighter ones if the hamstrings feel raw.

---

## (y-) Common Mistakes

- **Rotating the back hip open.** It buys a fake extra few inches and trains a range you cannot kick from.
- **Rounding the spine to reach further.** The number that matters is hip angle, not how close your nose is to your knee.
- **Ignoring the back leg entirely.** Half the split lives there.
- **Testing the split every single day.** Your range fluctuates with sleep, hydration, and fatigue. Measure once a week, not once a session.
- **Bouncing.** Still no.
