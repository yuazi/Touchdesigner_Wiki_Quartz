The spinning back kick (dwi chagi) is the first spinning kick everyone should learn. It is the highest-percentage spin in sparring: it travels in a straight line, it hits with the heel, and it generates the most raw power of any kick in the sport because your whole bodyweight drives behind a locked-out leg. Learn this before the flashy stuff.

Read the shared mechanics first: [[notes/taekwondo/spinning-kicks|(y-) The Complete Guide]].

---

## (y-) Mental Model

It is not a "kick" so much as a **backward thrust**. Think of driving your heel straight back into a wall behind you, like a horse kicking. The spin is only there to turn your back to the target so the heel has somewhere to go. Straight line in, straight line out.

---

## (y-) The Step-by-Step

Assume an orthodox sparring stance (left foot forward).

1. **Pivot the front foot.** Rotate on the ball of your lead (left) foot, turning your toes inward across your body. This starts your hips opening away from the target.
2. **Turn the head and shoulders.** Wind your upper body around so you begin to face away. Your back turns toward the opponent.
3. **Spot over the shoulder.** As your head comes around, your eyes lock the target over your right shoulder. See it before you fire.
4. **Chamber tight.** Pull the kicking (right) knee up and in toward your chest. A tight chamber means a fast, hidden kick.
5. **Thrust the heel.** Drive the heel in a straight line directly at the target, hips pushing behind it, leg extending fully. Strike with the heel, toes pointed down or to the side.
6. **Re-chamber and land.** Pull the leg back along the same line and set down facing the target, guard up.

---

## (y-) Key Details

- **Strike with the heel, not the sole.** The heel is the hard, safe striking surface. A flat sole is weak and risks your toes.
- **Keep the kick straight.** A back kick that arcs is a telegraphed roundhouse. The power and the speed both come from the straight line. Imagine a rail running from your heel to the target.
- **Do not over-rotate.** You only need to turn far enough to point your heel at the target. Spinning too far throws you past the line and kills your balance.
- **Lead with the hip.** The kicking-side hip drives toward the target. If your hip stays behind, you are pushing with a bent leg and no power.

## (y-) Progression Drills

| Stage | Drill                                                                                          |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | **Wall push.** Face away from a wall, chamber, and push your heel flat into it. Learn the line and the hip drive with no spin. |
| 2     | **Slow spin, no target.** Walk through all six steps at quarter speed. Groove the pivot-turn-spot-chamber sequence. |
| 3     | **Bag thrust.** Spin and drive the heel into a heavy bag. Feel the whole bodyweight land behind a locked leg. |
| 4     | **Paddle on cue.** Partner holds a paddle at chest height and calls it. You spin and hit on command, training the spot and reaction. |
| 5     | **Counter drill.** Partner steps in with pressure. You read the step and back-kick into their advance. This is its real job. |

---

## (y-) Where It Wins in Sparring

The spinning back kick is a **counter to forward pressure**. When an opponent rushes in, they are walking their own chest and stomach onto your heel. The kick meets their momentum head-on, and the combined force is brutal. It is the classic answer to a fighter who only knows how to march forward. It also chains beautifully off a fake: throw a roundhouse to turn their guard, then spin the back kick into the gap. See the [[notes/taekwondo/combinations|(y-) combinations]] that use dwi chagi.

## (y-) Common Mistakes

| Mistake                        | Fix                                                                    |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kicking blind                  | Spot over the shoulder before the leg fires, every time.               |
| Arcing the kick like a hook    | Chamber tight and thrust straight. Picture the rail to the target.     |
| Hitting with the sole          | Point the toes down or aside so the heel leads.                        |
| Falling forward on the landing | Re-chamber the leg and set down under your hips, not out in front.     |
| Winding up too big             | Keep the pivot small and sharp so the spin does not announce itself.   |
