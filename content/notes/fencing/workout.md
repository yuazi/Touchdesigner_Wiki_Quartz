What I do at home when there is no piste, no weights, and no excuses. Épée bodies are unusual: powerful asymmetric legs, a stubbornly strong forearm on the weapon side, an iron core, and the ability to hold a deep half-squat for nine minutes without falling apart. None of that requires a gym.

This is a reference list, not a programmed week. Pick exercises from each section, train 3 to 4 times per week, and prioritise the footwork-conditioning block in the days closest to a bout.

---

## (y-) Legs / Explosiveness

The lunge is the most demanding lower-body action in sport. Train the back-leg drive and the front-leg deceleration.

- **Walking lunges (forward, reverse, lateral)**: 3 sets of 10 per leg. Vary direction every set.
- **Bulgarian split squats**: 3 sets of 8 per leg, rear foot on a chair. Brutal but specific.
- **Jump squats**: 3 sets of 8. Explode up, land soft. The back-leg drive of a lunge in vertical form.
- **Wall sits in en garde**: 3 holds of 60 seconds. Sit against a wall in fencing posture, weapon arm out.
- **Single-leg calf raises**: 3 sets of 15 per leg. Front-foot stability for the recovery.

---

## (y-) Core / Anti-Rotation

Épée touches require the torso to stay rigid while the limbs move violently. Train the core to resist rotation, not just to create it.

- **Plank**: 3 holds of 60 seconds. The baseline.
- **Side plank**: 3 holds of 45 seconds per side. Asymmetric core endurance.
- **Deadbug**: 3 sets of 10 per side. Trains anti-rotation under leg movement.
- **Hollow body hold**: 3 holds of 30 seconds. Hips and shoulders off the floor.
- **Pallof press (towel against doorframe)**: 3 sets of 10 per side. Anti-rotation against external load.

---

## (y-) Posterior Chain / Pull

The back leg pushes the lunge; the back chain pulls you out of it. Most fencers under-train this.

- **Single-leg Romanian deadlifts (bodyweight)**: 3 sets of 8 per leg. Hamstring and glute under balance demand.
- **Glute bridges**: 3 sets of 15. Add single-leg variations once 15 feels easy.
- **Doorway rows (towel)**: 3 sets of 10. Loop a towel around a sturdy door handle, lean back, pull.
- **Superman holds**: 3 holds of 20 seconds. Lower back and posterior shoulder.

---

## (y-) Footwork Conditioning

The closest thing to fencing without a partner. Do these in a hallway or any clear strip of floor.

- **Advance-retreat ladders**: 3 advances, 3 retreats, repeat for 90 seconds. Rest 30. Five rounds.
- **Balestra-lunge intervals**: 5 balestra-lunges, full recovery between each. Rest 60 seconds. Four sets.
- **Lunge-recover-lunge**: 10 reps per side. No pause between the recovery and the next lunge.
- **Shadow fencing**: 3-minute rounds against an imaginary opponent. Vary tempo, throw real attacks, recover real distance. Three rounds, 60-second rest between.

---

## (y-) Weapon-Arm Grip

The forearm of your weapon hand will be the first thing to fail in a long bout. Most people skip this and pay for it in the eighth touch of a 15-point match.

- **Wrist circles**: 30 seconds each direction. Warm-up and finisher.
- **Finger curls (bodyweight)**: hang a towel over a doorframe, grip with one hand, curl the fingers closed and open. 3 sets of 15.
- **Towel squeezes**: roll a hand towel into a tight tube, squeeze for 30 seconds, rest 30, repeat 5 times.
- **Dead hangs**: hang from any bar (a door pull-up bar works) for 30 seconds. 3 sets. Builds grip and decompresses the shoulder.

---

## (y-) Putting It Together

A workable week:

| Day | Focus                                    |
| --- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Mon | Legs + Core                              |
| Tue | Footwork conditioning + Grip             |
| Wed | Rest or light stretching                 |
| Thu | Posterior chain + Core                   |
| Fri | Footwork conditioning + Grip             |
| Sat | Long shadow-fencing session (4-5 rounds) |
| Sun | Rest                                     |

Or pick 2-3 sections per session and stack them. The point is consistency, not perfection.
