Fencing is asymmetric, deep-lunge-heavy, and obsessively focused on one wrist. Stretching for épée is not about getting flexible for its own sake; it is about owning the range you actually use and protecting the joints that take the punishment.

## The Golden Rule of Timing

Same rule as every other explosive sport: dynamic before, static after.

- **Pre-Bout (Warm-up):** **Dynamic Stretching only.** Long static holds before explosive movement can drop muscle power for up to 20 minutes afterwards.
- **Post-Bout (Cool-down):** **Static & PNF Stretching.** Muscles are warm, the nervous system is tired, range-of-motion gains are real.

---

## 1. Dynamic Mobility (Pre-Bout)

Get the joints moving through their full range without holding any position. The goal is to wake the nervous system, not to extend the muscle.

- **Leg swings (front, side, circular):** 10 per direction per leg. Mimics the lunge and the recovery.
- **Lateral lunges:** 5 per side. Opens the adductors for deep-lunge recovery.
- **Hip circles:** 10 each direction. The lunge demands hip mobility in every plane.
- **Thoracic rotations (open book):** 5 per side. The blade arm reaches further when the upper back rotates freely.
- **Wrist circles and figure-8s:** 30 seconds. The first thing to seize up in a long bout.
- **High knees and butt kicks:** 30 seconds each. Blood flow and dynamic hamstring/quad activation.

---

## 2. PNF: The Fast-Track for Hip and Adductor Range

**Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation** is still the most effective protocol for opening range quickly. The lunge depth most épéeists want comes from the hip flexors and the adductors. Both respond well to PNF.

### Contract-Relax Protocol

1. Move into the stretch to a comfortable limit (e.g. a low lunge with the rear knee on the floor).
2. **Contract:** Push against the floor or a wall for **6 to 10 seconds** at 50 to 70 percent effort.
3. **Relax:** Exhale and immediately move deeper into the stretch for **20 seconds**.
4. Repeat 3 times per side.

The trick is the exhale on the deepening phase. The nervous system relaxes the muscle as you breathe out.

---

## 3. Static Holds (Post-Bout)

Once the body is warm and the bout is over. Hold each for 30 to 60 seconds, breathe through the discomfort, never bounce.

- **Couch stretch** (rear quad + hip flexor against a wall): 60s per side. This is the single most useful stretch for a fencer.
- **Pigeon pose**: 60s per side. Glute and external hip rotators.
- **Seated hamstring reach**: 45s per side. Both legs together, then single-leg.
- **Frog stretch / middle splits**: 60s. Adductors. Go slowly.
- **Thread the needle**: 45s per side. Shoulder and rotator cuff.
- **Wrist flexor / extensor stretch against a wall**: 30s per direction, both hands.

---

## 4. Épée-Specific Targets

| Muscle Group       | Why It Matters in Épée                              | Recommended Stretch              |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| **Hip flexors**    | Front-leg drive in the lunge, recovery depth         | Couch stretch / low lunge        |
| **Adductors**      | Recovery from a deep lunge, lateral footwork         | Frog / middle splits / PNF       |
| **Hamstrings**     | Back-leg extension, lunge length                     | Seated reach / PNF reach         |
| **Glutes**         | Stability through the lunge, balance in recovery     | Pigeon / figure-4                |
| **T-spine (upper back)** | Blade-arm reach, torso rotation                | Open book / thread the needle    |
| **Wrists / forearms** | Blade control, grip endurance                    | Wrist flexor + extensor holds    |
| **Calves / soleus** | Front-foot deceleration after a lunge               | Downward dog / step-edge calf stretch |

---

## 5. The Asymmetry Problem

Fencing builds one side of the body more than the other. The weapon-side hip flexor gets shortened from being the trailing leg in every lunge. The weapon-side forearm gets thicker, tighter, and less mobile than the other.

You do not need to balance this perfectly (you cannot, the sport itself is asymmetric), but you should stretch the dominant side twice as often as the non-dominant side, just to slow down the imbalance.

---

## Pro-Tips

- **Breathe.** Long exhales tell the nervous system the stretch is safe.
- **Frequency over intensity.** 10 minutes every day moves the needle further than an hour once a week.
- **Isometric strength at end-range.** Being able to hold your leg in a deep position is more useful than being able to passively flop into it.
- **Hydrate.** Dehydrated muscles are stiffer and tear more easily. Two glasses of water before a stretching session, one after.
