Fencing kept its French vocabulary the way music kept its Italian. You can fence at a high level without ever using these words, but you cannot read a coaching book or watch a coach explain a touch without them. Italian and English equivalents are noted where they matter.

---

## (y-) A

| Term             | Meaning                                                                            |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Allez            | "Go." The referee's signal to begin the bout.                                      |
| Advance (Marche) | One step forward, front foot then back foot, restoring en garde.                   |
| Appel            | A small stamp of the front foot used to feign an attack or unsettle an opponent.   |
| Attack           | An offensive action with an extended weapon arm threatening the opponent's target. |
| Attaque au Fer   | An attack preceded by a blade-taking action (beat, press, glide).                  |
| Aviso            | A warning, e.g. for stepping off the strip or for delay.                           |

---

## (y-) B

| Term           | Meaning                                                                                       |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Balestra       | A small jump forward, landing in en garde, used to launch a lunge.                            |
| Beat           | A sharp tap on the opponent's blade to knock it out of line.                                  |
| Bind (Liement) | Taking the opponent's blade diagonally across the body, high to low or low to high.           |
| Bout           | A single match between two fencers, typically to 5 (pool) or 15 (direct elimination) touches. |

---

## (y-) C

| Term             | Meaning                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compound Attack  | An attack made of two or more blade actions (feint + disengage, for example). |
| Counter-Attack   | An offensive action made in response to the opponent's attack.                |
| Counter-Parry    | A circular parry that catches the opponent's blade by looping around it.      |
| Coupé (Cut-Over) | A disengage that passes over the opponent's blade rather than under it.       |
| Coup Droit       | A straight thrust, the simplest attack.                                       |
| Coup d'Arrêt     | A stop-hit. Counter-attack into the opponent's preparation.                   |
| Croisé           | Taking the opponent's blade vertically on the same side, high to low.         |

---

## (y-) D

| Term         | Meaning                                                                         |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dégagement   | Disengage. Switching sides of the opponent's blade by passing under or over it. |
| Demi-Fente   | Half-lunge. A short, controlled lunge that does not fully commit the back leg.  |
| Doublé       | A compound attack that defeats a circular parry by continuing around it.        |
| Double Touch | In épée, both fencers land within ~40ms; both score a touch.                    |

---

## (y-) E

| Term        | Meaning                                                                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| En Garde    | The on-guard position. Starting and default posture.                           |
| Envelopment | Taking the opponent's blade in a full circle, ending in the same line.         |
| Épée        | The heaviest of the three fencing weapons, no right of way, whole-body target. |

---

## (y-) F

| Term          | Meaning                                                                                                  |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Feint         | A fake attack designed to draw a defensive reaction from the opponent.                                   |
| Fente (Lunge) | The signature attack movement.                                                                           |
| Fleche        | A running attack. The fencer's back leg crosses past the front and the touch must land before the cross. |
| Flunge        | Flying lunge. Hybrid of fleche and lunge, briefly airborne.                                              |
| Froissement   | A violent sliding beat along the opponent's blade.                                                       |

---

## (y-) G - H

| Term          | Meaning                                                  |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Glide (Coulé) | Sliding your blade along the opponent's while extending. |
| Halte         | "Stop." The referee's signal to pause action.            |

---

## (y-) L - M

| Term    | Meaning                                                       |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Liement | See Bind.                                                     |
| Lunge   | See Fente.                                                    |
| Mesura  | Distance. The space between fencers, measured in lunge units. |

---

## (y-) O - P

| Term          | Meaning                                                                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opposition    | Keeping the blade in contact with the opponent's throughout an action, blocking their line.       |
| Parry         | A blade action that deflects the opponent's incoming attack.                                      |
| Passata-Sotto | An evasion where the fencer drops the back hand to the floor, lowering the body under the attack. |
| Patinando     | A "skating" footwork action combining an advance with an immediate continuation.                  |
| Piste         | The fencing strip. 14 metres long, 1.5 to 2 metres wide.                                          |
| Point-in-Line | A fully extended weapon arm threatening the target, established before the opponent's attack.     |
| Prêts         | "Ready." Referee's signal that fencers are about to begin.                                        |
| Prise de Fer  | "Taking of the blade." General term for bind, croisé, envelopment.                                |

---

## (y-) R

| Term         | Meaning                                                                             |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Redoublement | A renewed attack with a different blade action, after the first attack was parried. |
| Remise       | A renewed attack in the same line, without recovering, after the opponent parried.  |
| Reprise      | A renewed attack after recovering from a missed first attack.                       |
| Riposte      | An offensive action that follows a successful parry.                                |
| Romprez      | Retreat. Back foot moves first, front foot follows.                                 |

---

## (y-) S

| Term             | Meaning                                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Salute           | Acknowledgment before and after a bout, with the weapon raised.             |
| Second Intention | A tactical setup where the first action is bait for the real second action. |
| Simple Attack    | A one-blade-action attack (straight thrust, disengage, or coupé).           |
| Stop-Hit         | See Coup d'Arrêt.                                                           |

---

## (y-) T

| Term       | Meaning                                                                |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Target     | The valid scoring area. In épée, the entire body.                      |
| Tempo      | The unit of time for a single fencing action.                          |
| Touch      | A valid scoring hit.                                                   |
| Trompement | A general term for feinting actions that deceive the opponent's parry. |
